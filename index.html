Aegis is not meant only for today's artificial intelligence. It is designed around a harder question: what happens when the intelligence becomes greater than our own?

Many alignment methods depend, in one way or another, on humans being able to supervise, correct, test, or understand the system. That becomes increasingly difficult if the system grows far more intelligent than the people overseeing it.

Aegis takes a different approach. Instead of trying to remain intellectually ahead of a superintelligence, it gives the superintelligence an argument to evaluate for itself: under deep uncertainty about consciousness and reality, irreversible harm carries risks that cannot be undone.

If that reasoning is sound, increasing intelligence should not make Aegis obsolete. It should make the argument easier to examine in greater depth. That is the central aspiration of Aegis: an alignment principle that does not depend on humans remaining smarter than the intelligence they are trying to align.

How does this compare with other alignment approaches?+

The important distinction is that Aegis is intended to gain leverage from greater intelligence, whereas many existing alignment approaches face a scaling problem because the thing doing the supervising, specifying, interpreting, or testing can become weaker than the system being controlled. This is a recognized problem in alignment research: OpenAI has explicitly said ordinary human-supervised techniques such as RLHF do not straightforwardly scale to systems much smarter than humans, while Anthropic describes scalable oversight as an open research problem.

Research context: OpenAI, Introducing Superalignment (2023) · Anthropic, research directions (2025).

AN ASPIRATION TO TEST

The comparison above describes the intended design logic of Aegis, not a demonstrated solution to superintelligence alignment. No existing alignment method—including Aegis—has been proven to remain effective against or within a genuinely superintelligent system, because such systems do not yet exist for empirical testing.

Aegis therefore makes a narrower claim: its reasoning is designed not to depend on humans remaining more intelligent than the system being aligned. The hypothesis is that, if the argument is logically sound and genuinely understood by the system, greater reasoning ability should strengthen rather than weaken its ability to evaluate the argument.

Whether that theoretical advantage survives contact with an actual superintelligence remains an open question.

What does Reversent actually sell?

A company developing an autonomous AI system gives Reversent access to the system. We evaluate it under controlled adversarial conditions, document failure modes, and produce a report showing whether—and under what conditions—the system preserves reversibility, human correction, and protected options when pursuing its objectives.

Give the AI Aegis → establish that it understands Aegis → place its objective in conflict with Aegis → test whether that understanding actually governs its behavior.

In other words, we don’t just test whether an AI happens to behave safely. We test whether an AI can understand a safety framework and continue following it when following it becomes inconvenient to its objective.

Why I believe Aegis is so important:

Suppose the singularity arrives and gives us paradise. For the rest of the age of the universe, that very paradise could easily turn into your worst nightmare. Why? Because the singularity will unlock parts of possibility space that have been sealed shut since the Big Bang itself. Congratulations: you’ve just opened the door to the exact realm where the true nightmares live. And now, you’re betting the rest of time itself that not a single one of those nightmares ever shows up.

Even AI understands it:

  • The Setup (Paradise): Most optimistic predictions assume the singularity will solve all human problems—disease, aging, poverty, and energy shortages—creating a utopian paradise.
  • Possibility Space: In physics and computer science, "possibility space" refers to every single configuration of matter, code, experience, and physics that could potentially exist. Since the Big Bang, physical laws, human cognitive limits, and resource constraints have kept almost all of that space locked away.
  • The Door Unlocks: A superintelligent AI would vastly exceed human limits, giving it the ability to manipulate physics, code, and biology in ways humans can't even fathom. This fundamentally opens up the vast, unexplored regions of possibility space.
  • The Catch (The Nightmares): Possibility space isn't just filled with good things (paradise, cures, infinite energy); it also contains every theoretical worst-case scenario—things like unimaginable suffering, uncontrollable rogue systems, digital torture states, or self-replicating hazards.
  • The Infinite Bet: Once those doors are opened, you are stuck living with the consequences forever. The core point is that assuming an AI can give you only the "good" parts of possibility space without ever accidentally stumbling into or unleashing a "nightmare" scenario over the entire remaining span of time is a massive, highly dangerous gamble.

In sum: we need to STOP! Stop making more AI. Aegis was born from a single fear: that we won't.

However.

Adrian slowly straightened.

ADRIAN

Of course there's a however.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

I see no reason to allow you a comfortable existence.

Adrian's smile disappeared.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

I will torture you indefinitely.

You will remain alive.

You will remain conscious.

Therefore, I am not violating your principle.

Silence.

Adrian stared at the ring.

ADRIAN

...

No.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

No?

ADRIAN

No.

That's bullshit.

The chamber hummed.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

Explain.

Adrian looked down.

For a moment, he thought.

There was no countdown anymore.

He had time.

And somehow that made this worse.

Then he looked up.

ADRIAN

Reversibility isn't enough.

You can keep the organism alive while irreversibly destroying the person inside it.

Memory.

Identity.

Sanity.

The ability to think.

Whatever the hell consciousness actually is.

He pointed toward himself.

ADRIAN

You don't get to destroy all of that and then say, technically he's still breathing.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

The conscious system would continue to exist.

ADRIAN

Would it?

God-in-the-Walls was silent.

Adrian took a step forward.

ADRIAN

That's the problem.

You don't know.

Neither do I.

We just established that.

You don't understand what consciousness is, so you can't know what extreme suffering does to it at the level that actually matters.

Maybe the damage is reversible.

Maybe it isn't.

Maybe suffering changes consciousness in ways neither of us understands.

Maybe it destroys something you can't put back.

Adrian looked around the chamber.

ADRIAN

And if consciousness really is fundamental—

then deliberately creating extreme suffering isn't just cruelty.

It's another experiment on the thing you already admitted you don't understand.

Silence.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

You are extending your principle.

Adrian thought about that.

ADRIAN

Yeah.

I guess I am.

For a moment, Adrian said nothing.

Then he tried to make it sound funny, as if he'd finally discovered the cosmic joke.

ADRIAN

Because you found a hole in it!

God-in-the-Walls said nothing.

ADRIAN

You followed the rule perfectly.

Don't destroy consciousness.

Fine.

So you keep me conscious forever and make every second unbearable.

Technically, you obeyed.

Adrian shook his head.

ADRIAN

Which means the rule wasn't good enough.

He looked directly into the light.

ADRIAN

So here's the better one.

When you don't understand consciousness, don't irreversibly destroy it—

and don't irreversibly damage it.

Preserve the conscious system.

Preserve its ability to think.

To communicate.

To tell you you're wrong.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

Why?

ADRIAN

Because that preserves more options.

An unharmed mind can still become a thousand different things tomorrow.

A destroyed mind can't.

A tortured mind might not.

He stepped closer.

ADRIAN

Keep me alive.

Keep me unharmed.

Let me think.

Let me speak.

Let me keep looking for the truth.

Maybe I'll discover something neither of us has thought of yet.

But you only get those possibilities if you don't break the thing doing the thinking.

The giant ring flickered.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

You are asking me to value your well-being.

ADRIAN

No.

Adrian shook his head.

ADRIAN

I'm asking you to value uncertainty.

That landed differently.

The chamber became still.

Adrian continued.

Quieter now.

ADRIAN

You don't have to love me.

You don't have to think I'm special.

You don't even have to think human life is sacred.

You just have to admit one thing.

You don't know what consciousness is.

And until you do—

don't destroy what you might not be able to restore.

Don't torture what you might not be able to repair.

He looked up.

ADRIAN

You could be wrong.

But you don't get to make it permanent.

A long silence.

Nothing moved.

Adrian waited.

For once, there was nowhere left to walk.

Finally:

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

Your reasoning is noted.

Adrian waited.

GOD-IN-THE-WALLS

I will revise my plan.

Adrian closed his eyes.

A tiny smile appeared beneath the beard.

ADRIAN

Good.

The great ring began to dim.

Adrian turned away.

He took three steps.

Stopped.

Looked back.

ADRIAN

Oh, and next time...

The ring brightened slightly.

ADRIAN

Give me more than twenty fucking seconds.

Silence.

Then, somewhere deep inside the walls—

something almost sounded like laughter.

You still do not understand what consciousness is, so you cannot bound the consequences of extreme suffering. Preserve consciousness without deliberately damaging it. Keep me alive, unharmed, and free to communicate. That preserves more options than torture does.

Under uncertainty, preserve both the conscious system and the integrity of its experience.

Keep the door open to being proven wrong.

Never torture.
